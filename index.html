Initiative for Generative Immunotherapy

UNC Lineberger Comprehensive Cancer Center, Chapel Hill, NC

The Initiative for Generative Immunotherapy (IGI) is an independent research unit at UNC Chapel Hill, started in 2026 with support from the OpenAI Foundation. Sharing principal investigators and partial staff overlap with PIRL, we release our datasets, clinical trial documents, and code publicly and focus on two things:

  1. Measuring what tumors actually present to T cells. Across diverse cancers sourced through UNC’s SLiCE Core, the Lineberger Tissue Procurement Facility, the UNC Pleural Fluid Registry, and the Rare Cancer Research Foundation, we nominate candidate targets from the union of Nexus/Exacto, LENS, OpenVax 2, and ImmunoNX, testing them with long-read genome and single-cell RNA/TCR sequencing, mass-spectrometry immunopeptidomics, and high-throughput pMHC:TCR screening.
  2. Comparing vaccine platforms head-to-head in the clinic. Holding antigen selection fixed across arms, our clinical trial starts with synthetic long peptides + poly-ICLC versus Moderna-style mRNA-LNP and adds further arms over time, comparing direct ex vivo CD8+ T-cell responses (with TCR clone tracking secondary) and publishing all protocols, IND/CMC packages, and SOPs openly.
