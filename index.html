Initiative for Generative Immunotherapy

UNC Lineberger Comprehensive Cancer Center, Chapel Hill, NC

The Initiative for Generative Immunotherapy (IGI) is an independent research unit at UNC Chapel Hill, started in 2026 with support from the OpenAI Foundation. We work alongside the Personalized Immunotherapy Research Lab (PIRL) and build on OpenVax, the open-source toolkit for personalized cancer vaccine design maintained and actively developed into OpenVax 2 at PIRL.

We release everything we generate publicly (datasets, clinical trial documents, and code), except germline genetic information (DNA from patient or tumor samples). Our work focuses on two things:

  1. Measuring what tumors actually present to T cells. Across a broad set of cancer types with UNC’s SLiCE core and clinical biobanks, we pair long-read genome and single-cell RNA/TCR sequencing with mass-spectrometry immunopeptidomics and high-throughput pMHC:TCR screening to deorphanize tumor-specific TCRs from TILs and tumor-associated T cells.
  2. Comparing vaccine platforms head-to-head in the clinic. Holding antigen selection fixed across arms (combining LENS, OpenVax 2, PIRL tools such as Exacto and Nexus, and standard workflows like ImmunoNX), our clinical trial starts with synthetic long peptides + poly-ICLC versus Moderna-style mRNA-LNP and adds further arms over time, comparing direct ex vivo CD8+ T-cell responses (with TCR clone tracking secondary) and publishing all protocols, IND/CMC packages, and SOPs openly.

As a smaller exploratory effort, we also develop targeted lipid nanoparticles (tLNPs) to deliver discovered TCRs as in vivo TCR-Ts or, with dendritic-cell targeting, to deliver antigens as vaccines.
