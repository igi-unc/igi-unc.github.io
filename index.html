Initiative for Generative Immunotherapy

Personalized cancer vaccines can fail for two different reasons: the predicted targets are not actually presented on the tumor, or the vaccine does not raise enough CD8+ T cells against them. Past trials have tangled those questions together. IGI separates them—and releases everything we generate openly, except germline-identifying DNA from patients or tumors.

Independent research unit at UNC Chapel Hill, launched in 2026 with support from the OpenAI Foundation (announcement) alongside PIRL.

01

What we do

01.A // DATA

Measure what tumors actually present—and which T cells recognize it

Most predicted neoantigens never appear at meaningful levels on the tumor surface. Across a broad set of cancer types—working closely with UNC’s Screening Live Cancer Explants (SLiCE) core for viable surgical tissue alongside clinical specimen collections—we pair long-read tumor/normal genome sequencing and single-cell full-length RNA/TCR sequencing with multi-tier mass-spectrometry immunopeptidomics across classical mutations, structural-variant reading frames, fusions, and cancer-testis antigens.

In parallel, we run high-throughput pMHC:TCR screening to deorphanize tumor-specific TCRs from tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and tumor-associated T cells, linking presented targets directly to functional T-cell recognition.

01.B // TRIAL

Compare vaccine platforms head-to-head and publish the entire trial

Holding the antigen-selection pipeline constant lets a clinical trial isolate vaccine potency. We are starting with synthetic long peptides + poly-ICLC as the baseline arm against a Moderna-style mRNA-LNP vaccine, and adding more arms over time. Every arm is judged on the same primary yardstick: direct ex vivo CD8+ T-cell responses, with longitudinal TCR clone tracking in blood as a secondary outcome.

Just as important as the immunogenicity readout is making the clinical trial process legible and reusable: we are publishing our clinical protocols, IND and CMC packages, regulatory correspondence, and SOPs openly on GitHub so other groups can launch and iterate on AI-designed trials.

EXPLORATORY

Targeted RNA delivery. As a secondary effort alongside data generation and the platform trial, we are developing targeted lipid nanoparticles (tLNPs)—either to deliver discovered TCRs as in vivo TCR-Ts, or with dendritic-cell targeting to deliver antigens as vaccines.

02

Open data

Open by default. Everything we generate—immunopeptidomics spectra, validated pMHCs, somatic target and isoform calls, gene-expression matrices, TCR repertoires, deorphanized pMHC:TCR pairs, immune readouts, and clinical trial filings—is released publicly without restriction. Only germline-identifying genetic information (DNA from patient or tumor samples) is held in controlled-access archives.

Artifact Contents Access & Archive
pMHC presentation Quantitative MS immunopeptidomics spectra & validated targets across diverse tumors OPEN · PRIDE · IEDB · GitHub
TCRs & T-cell readouts Deorphanized TIL & vaccine-elicited pMHC:TCR pairs, TCR repertoires, direct ex vivo CD8+ assays OPEN · VDJdb · AIRR · GitHub

Trial paperwork Clinical protocols, IND & CMC packages, SOPs, and regulatory correspondence OPEN · GitHub

03

Code & benchmarks

igi-unc/igi-syn-seq
Generator and truth specifications for IGI-SYN-SEQ-01 and IGI-SYN-SEQ-02: synthetic 15-assay tumor/normal datasets (Illumina, PacBio HiFi/Kinnex, ONT, 10x GEX/TCR) for benchmarking antigen discovery across every variant class.
igi-unc/igi-lims
Chain-of-custody specimen database tracking tube-to-tube provenance from tissue collection through cellular fractions, DNA/RNA extracts, peptide eluates, QC imports, and sequencing files.
igi-unc/igi-data-librarian
Sequencing registry and Slack automation for dataset accessioning, tube verification, and automated Nextflow / RAFT quality-control runs.

Companion open-source stacks: PIRL (@pirl-unc) — LENS, RAFT, mhcgnomes · OpenVax (@openvax) — MHCflurry, Vaxrank, Isovar, Varcode, Topiary.
