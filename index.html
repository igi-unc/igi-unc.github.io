UNC Lineberger Comprehensive Cancer Center

Initiative for Generative Immunotherapy

We generate open, multimodal datasets from human tumors and the T cells that recognize them—pairing short- and long-read sequencing, single-cell profiling, MHC immunopeptidomics, and clinical vaccine trials so computational models can learn which tumor targets provoke an effective immune response.

Launched in 2026 at the UNC School of Medicine with a $40M grant from the OpenAI Foundation’s Public Data for Health program (announcement), in close collaboration with the Personalized Immunotherapy Research Lab (PIRL).

Approach

Personalized cancer vaccines direct a patient’s immune system against somatic mutations and other tumor-specific abnormalities. Despite encouraging clinical signals, vaccine target selection still relies heavily on calling variants from tumor DNA and RNA and inferring which peptides reach the cell surface and engage T cells.

Closing the gap between predicted candidates and true immunogenic targets requires paired measurements on the same specimens—observing the tumor genome, full-length transcript isoforms, MHC-eluted surface peptides, and functional T-cell responses together rather than in disconnected cohorts.

Open tumor–immune datasets

Deep multimodal profiling across hundreds of tumors, coupling short- and long-read DNA/RNA sequencing and single-cell TCR/expression data with direct mass-spectrometry measurements of HLA-presented peptides, released for the broader research community.

Comparative vaccine trials

Clinical studies testing therapeutic cancer vaccine platforms and dosing strategies head-to-head—including in triple-negative breast cancer—with standardized longitudinal measurements of T-cell expansion and specificity.

Benchmarks & generative models

Known-truth synthetic multi-assay benchmarks and machine learning models trained on paired presentation and reactivity data to select neoantigens, splice variants, fusions, endogenous retroelements, and other non-canonical targets.

Code & Benchmarks

Active IGI engineering, data infrastructure, and benchmark generation live in the igi-unc GitHub organization alongside our open-source antigen discovery ecosystems.

IGI repositories

igi-syn-seq

Generator and specification for IGI-SYN-SEQ-01 and IGI-SYN-SEQ-02: synthetic, known-truth tumor/normal datasets spanning 15 sample types (Illumina WES, WGS, bulk RNA, 10x 5′ GEX and TCR; PacBio HiFi and Kinnex; Oxford Nanopore WGS and RNA) to benchmark antigen callers across SNVs, indels, structural variants, fusions, splice isoforms, ERVs, CTAs, and viral integrations.

Source & design docs

igi-lims

Chain-of-custody specimen database and web interface for IGI biospecimens. Tracks parent-to-child lineage from primary collections through cellular fractions, pellets, DNA/RNA extracts, and MHC peptide eluates to instrument QC imports and sequencing files.

Source

igi-data-librarian

Sequencing registry and Slack workflow automation for dataset accessioning, LIMS tube verification, and automated quality-control runs via RAFT and Nextflow.

Source

Companion ecosystems

PIRL Software

Home of LENS (Landscape of Effective Neoantigens Software), RAFT (reproducible Nextflow workflow manager), and mhcgnomes, developed by the Personalized Immunotherapy Research Lab at UNC.

OverviewGitHub (@pirl-unc)

OpenVax

Open-source Python libraries for neoantigen discovery, MHC presentation modeling, and vaccine peptide selection, including MHCflurry, Vaxrank, Isovar, Varcode, and Topiary.
