Open Data

Tumor antigen presentation, TCR specificity, and public archives

Computational pipelines nominate many candidate tumor antigens from sequencing reads, but only a small minority are presented on tumor MHC molecules at levels T cells can recognize. IGI is generating an open reference dataset that pairs deep tumor sequencing with direct measurements of peptide-MHC presentation and T-cell recognition across diverse solid, hematologic, and rare fusion-driven cancers.

Specimens & candidate sourcing

  • Biobanks & tissue protocols: viable surgical tumor tissue from UNC’s Screening Live Cancer Explants (SLiCE) Core, solid and hematologic repositories in the UNC Lineberger Tissue Procurement Facility alongside institutional bone-marrow protocols, malignant effusions and matched blood from the UNC Pleural Fluid Registry, and surgical specimens from rare cancers via the Rare Cancer Research Foundation.
  • Candidate antigen pool: rather than testing a single algorithm’s top picks, we draw candidate targets from the union of Nexus/Exacto, LENS, OpenVax 2, and ImmunoNX (see Code).

Measurements

  • Long-read genome & single-cell RNA/TCR sequencing: tumor/normal whole-genome sequencing and full-length single-cell transcriptomes + paired αβ TCRs spanning point mutations, indels, structural variants, fusions, splice isoforms, and cancer-testis antigens.
  • Mass-spectrometry immunopeptidomics: targeted heavy-isotope standards, inclusion-list DDA, and personalized-proteome DIA to quantify which candidate pMHCs appear on the tumor surface.
  • High-throughput pMHC:TCR screening: functional deorphanization of tumor-specific TCRs from tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and tumor-associated T cells.

Release policy & archives

Everything we generate is released publicly without restriction, with one exception: germline genetic information (DNA and germline-carrying sequencing reads from patient normal or tumor samples), which goes to controlled-access archives to protect patient privacy.
